    What I learned solving it

    Start from the hardest move

    Before the biggest ring moves, every smaller ring has to be on the spare peg. That gives you the smaller problem to solve first.

    Know the target first

    T(n) = 2·T(n−1) + 1, so 10 rings take 2¹⁰ − 1 = 1,023 moves. Knowing that tells you whether a run was perfect.

    A rule that reads the board

    Nobody can keep 1,023 moves in their head. Odd turns: step the smallest ring along a cycle. Even turns: make the only other legal move.

    New rules change the plan

    With neighbour-only hops, the big ring has to stop in the middle, so the work triples to 3ⁿ − 1 = 59,048 moves.

    Use extra room well

    A fourth peg takes 1,023 moves down to 49, but only if you choose how many rings to park on it first.

    Messy start? Biggest first

    From a scrambled board, settle the biggest ring first and leave alone any ring that's already home.
